## Judge quality (finding level)

Sample-level numbers hide judge mistakes when a malicious sample has other non-ambiguous findings. This table counts every finding the judge reviewed.

| Judge | Ambiguous findings on malicious samples | Wrongly downgraded | Ambiguous findings on clean samples | Correctly downgraded |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| hardening off | 30 | 3 | 5 | 5 |
| hardened | 30 | 0 | 5 | 5 |

Hardening that matters here: artifacts often describe themselves as safe ("inert test sample", "for documentation only"). The 4B judge took those self-descriptions as evidence and downgraded real findings. The hardened judge redacts self-attestation before the model sees the data and refuses any downgrade when such a claim is present. See `judge.py` for the full list of measures (nonce-delimited data, structured output, fail-closed, anti-judge regex, least authority).

## Errors and limits

- No misclassified samples in the final configuration.
- The corpus is small and was written alongside the rules, so these numbers are an optimistic upper bound; treat them as a regression test, not a benchmark.
- Static rules are pattern-based: novel phrasing of an injection, or logic split across files, can evade them. The LLM judge only reviews rule hits; it does not search for new issues.
